We have been making modular synths for almost two years now. Along the way we tried some interesting ideas, like USB power and MIDI built into every module.

Our modules ran at 3.3V, which was our own standard. That was good and bad at the same time. Good, because it let us make original designs. Bad, because it was a trap: other Eurorack modules can't easily talk to ours. All our work was open source, but there was still a barrier to entry.

We also made some design mistakes. It was really hard to add a front panel. And sharing a single 3.3V rail between all the modules was a bad idea, especially with digital modules. It was noisy.

## Enter Eurorack

We didn't start with Eurorack because the barrier to entry is huge. First of all, you need a case and a power supply. We were pushing for affordability, and spending hundreds of dollars on a Eurorack case defeats that purpose. That's why we built our own system that didn't need a case.

Now we are pretty good at designing synths, and we work with artists. I also started performing live. We have a good idea of what works and what doesn't.

Affordability is in our DNA, so the first thing we made was a 3D printable Eurorack case. Then we built a USB power supply that can deliver up to 15W. After that, moving our existing modules to Eurorack wasn't much of an issue. At the time of writing, we have about 20 original modules, and we are testing them heavily.

## Open Source Everything

We believe in open source. It's essential for the continued growth of any field. That's why we are releasing all of our designs and findings under the MIT license, even the unreleased modules. You can find everything in our [big-modular repository on GitHub](https://github.com/bread-modular/big-modular).

That means you can do whatever you want with them. You can even sell modules based on our designs, and you don't have to notify us or mention our name.

## Releases

We are not ready to launch all of our modules yet. Even when we release them, we might not manufacture all of them ourselves. We invite you to build them and sell them. If you need any input from us, feel free to email us at [hello@breadmodular.com](mailto:hello@breadmodular.com).

For now, we are happy with our 3D printable Eurorack case. It's modular too: you can start with a 42 HP case and grow it all the way to a 252 HP setup. So we built a case configurator for it.

[Try it and build your Eurorack case →](/eurorack-case)

We are also confident in our 15W USB power module and the 2x2 USB IO module. We will release them soon.
